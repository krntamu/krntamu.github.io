Equality-constrained optimization

Let

x_=[x1x2].

We want to solve

maxx_f(x_)subject toh(x_)=0,

where f is the objective function and h=0 defines the constraint curve.

The gradient ∇h is perpendicular to the constraint curve. Therefore, a tangent direction d_ satisfies

(∇h(x_))Td_=0.

At a constrained extremum, the objective has no first-order change in a feasible tangent direction:

(∇f(x_))Td_=0.

Otherwise, moving in one of the two tangent directions would improve the objective.

Thus both gradients are perpendicular to the tangent. Assuming differentiability and ∇h≠0_, they must be parallel:

∇f(x_)=λ∇h(x_).

The gradients point in the same direction when λ>0, and in opposite directions when λ<0.

The Lagrangian

Define

ℒ(x_,λ)=f(x_)−λh(x_).

Setting its derivatives to zero gives

∇x_ℒ=∇f−λ∇h=0_,∂ℒ∂λ=−h=0.

These equations enforce both the parallel-gradient condition and feasibility. Their solutions are candidate extrema; comparing objective values identifies the maximum.

Example 1: Linear objective

Maximize

f(x_)=x1+2x2subject toh(x_)=x12+x22−4=0.

Construct the Lagrangian:

ℒ=x1+2x2−λ(x12+x22−4).

Take the derivatives and set them to zero:

∂ℒ∂x1=1−2λx1=0∂ℒ∂x2=2−2λx2=0∂ℒ∂λ=−(x12+x22−4)=0

The first two equations give

x1=12λ,x2=1λ=2x1.

Substituting into the constraint,

54λ2=4⇒λ=±54.

The positive multiplier gives f=25; the negative multiplier gives f=−25. Therefore, the maximum is

x1⋆=25,x2⋆=45,λ⋆=54,fmax=25.

Geometrically, the highest objective contour that touches the circle is tangent to it at x_⋆.

Example 2: Nonlinear objective

Maximize

f(x_)=x12x2subject toh(x_)=x12+x22−4=0.

Construct the Lagrangian:

ℒ=x12x2−λ(x12+x22−4).

Take the derivatives and set them to zero:

∂ℒ∂x1=2x1(x2−λ)=0∂ℒ∂x2=x12−2λx2=0∂ℒ∂λ=−(x12+x22−4)=0

The first equation gives two cases.

Case 1: x1=0.

The constraint gives x2=±2, and the second equation gives λ=0. Both points have objective value 0.

Case 2: x1≠0.

The first equation gives λ=x2. Substituting into the second,

x12=2x22.

The constraint then becomes

3x22=4.

Hence

x2=±23,x1=±83,λ=x2.

The objective values are

f(x_)=83x2=±1633.

Comparing all candidates, the maximum occurs at two points:

x1⋆=±83,x2⋆=23,λ⋆=23,fmax=1633.

Both maximizers have the same value because the objective depends on x12. At each one, ∇f=λ⋆∇h, so the objective gradient has no component along the feasible tangent.

For both examples, continuity on the compact circle guarantees global extrema. Also, ∇h=2x_≠0_ everywhere on the circle, so every extremum is among the candidates above.
