03

The PCA optimization problem

Maximize the variance retained
by the projection.

First subtract the sample mean. In this derivation, xᵢ denotes a centered sample, so ∑ᵢ xᵢ = 0. Define the empirical covariance matrix

R = 1N − 1 ∑i=1N xᵢxᵢᴴ.

For our dataset, N = 100. Here ᴴ means conjugate transpose; for real data it is simply ᵀ. All variances below are computed from the samples using the same 1/(N−1) normalization.

The lower-dimensional projection and its reconstruction

Choose a unit vector a, with aᴴa = 1. Each data vector is represented by a single coordinate:

zᵢ = aᴴxᵢ.

zᵢ is the lower-dimensional projection: one scalar for each original vector. The corresponding reconstruction in the original space is

x̂ᵢ = azᵢ = aaᴴxᵢ.

The reconstruction lies on the line spanned by a. The residual xᵢ − x̂ᵢ is perpendicular to that line. For an observation before centering, add the sample mean back to its reconstruction.

TWO EQUIVALENT OBJECTIVES

Keep the most variance, or lose the least.

The projected coordinates are centered because ∑ᵢ zᵢ = 0. Their sample variance is aᴴRa. Orthogonal projection splits the total sample variance into retained and residual parts:

1N − 1 ∑i=1N ‖xᵢ − x̂ᵢ‖₂² = tr(R) − aᴴRa.

Since tr(R) is fixed for the dataset, these two problems have the same solution:

MAXIMIZE RETAINED VARIANCE
maxaᴴa=1 aᴴRa
⇔
MINIMIZE RECONSTRUCTION ERROR
minaᴴa=1 1N − 1 ∑ᵢ ‖xᵢ − aaᴴxᵢ‖₂²

We will solve the maximization problem directly.

1

Maximize the projected sample variance.

Using zᵢ = aᴴxᵢ, the objective becomes

var(z) = 1N − 1 ∑i=1N |zᵢ|² = aᴴRa.
maxaᴴa=1 aᴴRa.

The unit-length constraint is essential: otherwise, rescaling a could increase the objective without bound.

2

Apply a Lagrange multiplier.

ℒ(a, λ) = aᴴRa − λ(aᴴa − 1).

For real vectors, the stationarity condition is 2Ra − 2λa = 0. For complex vectors, differentiating with respect to a* gives the same eigenvector equation:

Ra = λa.

Thus a stationary direction is an eigenvector of R. Multiplying by aᴴ shows that the projected sample variance in that direction equals λ.

3

Select the eigenvector with the largest eigenvalue.

R is Hermitian positive semidefinite, so it has an orthonormal eigenbasis with λ₁ ≥ λ₂ ≥ ⋯ ≥ λₙ ≥ 0. Expand any unit vector as a = ∑ⱼ cⱼvⱼ, with ∑ⱼ|cⱼ|² = 1. Then

aᴴRa = ∑ⱼ λⱼ|cⱼ|² ≤ λ₁ ∑ⱼ|cⱼ|² = λ₁.

The upper bound is attained by a = v₁. Therefore the first principal component is an eigenvector of the empirical covariance R corresponding to its largest eigenvalue, and the maximum retained variance is λ₁.

Why do the top k eigenvectors maximize retained variance?

The total sample variance of the k-dimensional projected data is

1N − 1 ∑i=1N ‖Uᴴxᵢ‖₂² = tr(UᴴRU).

So the higher-dimensional PCA problem is

maxUᴴU=Iₖ tr(UᴴRU).

Write R = VΛVᴴ and set B = VᴴU. Then BᴴB = Iₖ. Define wⱼ as the sum of squared magnitudes in row j of B. These weights satisfy 0 ≤ wⱼ ≤ 1 and ∑ⱼwⱼ = k, so

tr(UᴴRU) = ∑ⱼ λⱼwⱼ ≤ ∑j=1k λⱼ.

Shifting weight from a smaller eigenvalue to an unfilled larger one cannot decrease the objective. Its largest value is attained when the first k weights are 1. Therefore an optimal choice is Uₖ = [v₁, …, vₖ], the eigenvectors of R corresponding to its k largest eigenvalues. The optimal subspace retains total variance λ₁ + ⋯ + λₖ.

EXPLAINED VARIANCE RATIO
(λ₁ + ⋯ + λₖ) / tr(R)
RESIDUAL SAMPLE VARIANCE
tr(R) − ∑j=1k λⱼ = λₖ₊₁ + ⋯ + λₙ

The residual sample variance is (1/(N−1)) ∑ᵢ ‖xᵢ − UₖUₖᴴxᵢ‖₂². If reconstruction error is averaged with 1/N instead, multiply this value by (N−1)/N; the optimal subspace is unchanged.

For the original observations, subtract the sample mean x̄ before projecting and add it back after reconstructing. PCA here uses the empirical covariance R, so features keep their original scale; standardizing features changes the problem.

A note about uniqueness. Eigenvector signs (or complex phases) can change without changing the subspace. Repeated eigenvalues allow different orthonormal bases; a tie at the k-th eigenvalue can make the optimal subspace nonunique. With zero total variance, every direction is optimal and the explained variance ratio is undefined.
