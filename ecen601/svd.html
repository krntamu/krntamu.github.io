The decomposition, explicitly

H means conjugate transpose. For real matrices, VH = VT. Singular values are nonnegative and sorted from largest to smallest. Bases can differ by signs or phases; repeated singular values also permit different orthonormal bases. Rank is numerical, using a floating-point tolerance.

The sum of outer products

A = ∑i=1r σiuiviH

Each column of U pairs with the corresponding conjugate-transposed column of V. Multiplying by its singular value gives one m × n matrix; adding these matrices recovers A. Only numerically nonzero singular values are included.

03

A practical example: movie preferences

6 users × 5 movies

Rows are users; columns are movies. These fictional, complete ratings use a 1–5 scale, where 5 means a strong preference. The user profiles and genre labels help us interpret the patterns; only the numbers enter the SVD.

Start with ratings, then remove each user’s baseline

Alex favors science fiction, Casey favors romance, and Emery favors animation. Some users also rate everything more generously than others. To study relative preferences, subtract each user’s own mean rating from every entry in that row.

On a narrow screen, scroll the tables horizontally to see all five movies.

Cij = Rij − μi,   C = R − μ1T = UΣVT

Here μi is user i’s mean across the five movies, and 1 is a five-entry column of ones. For Alex, μ₁ = 3, so [5, 5, 1, 2, 2] becomes [2, 2, −2, −1, −1]. A positive centered rating means “above this user’s usual rating,” and a negative one means “below it.”

Inspect the numeric SVD of the centered 6 × 5 matrix

Rank-compact form: C = UrΣrVrT, with dimensions (6 × 4)(4 × 4)(4 × 5). The full form uses U ∈ ℝ6 × 6, Σ ∈ ℝ6 × 5, and V ∈ ℝ5 × 5.

What do the left and right singular vectors mean?

For component k, the right singular vector vk ∈ ℝ⁵ gives one weight per movie: a unit-length direction in movie space. The left singular vector uk ∈ ℝ⁶ gives one weight per user: a unit-length pattern across users. The singular value σk tells us how strongly these two patterns are coupled.

Cvk = σkuk,   CTuk = σkvk

The first identity is especially useful: take a user’s centered row and dot it with the movie vector. The result is σkuik, that user’s score along the movie direction. The entries of uk are normalized user weights; the scaled entries σkuik are the corresponding scores. These are different quantities.

These directions are learned from the fictional numbers, so a component can mix genres. They are neither genre labels nor probabilities. Flipping both uk and vk changes their displayed signs but leaves σkukvkT unchanged. Interpret the paired signs and contrasts together.

Build the ratings back up, one preference pattern at a time

Cq = ∑k=1q σkukvkT,   R̂q = μ1T + Cq

Each outer product contributes a full 6 × 5 preference pattern. A user and movie with weights of the same sign get a positive contribution; opposite signs give a negative contribution. Add the user’s mean back only after summing the centered components.

At q = 0, every movie receives that user’s mean. At q = 4, all original ratings are recovered, up to rounding. Approximate entries are continuous and can fall outside 1–5; no rounding to stars or clipping is applied.

See this component’s full weighted outer product

Why only four components? Every centered row sums to zero, so C1 = 0. The five movie columns are dependent: rank(C) ≤ 4, and this example attains rank 4. The fifth singular value is zero and does not describe an additional preference pattern.

This is a demonstration using complete fictional data. The reconstruction compresses known ratings; it does not evaluate recommendations for unseen movies or handle missing ratings. This example has its own fixed dataset and does not change either matrix input above.
