Image compression: algorithm and storage
  1. Build the image matrix. Resize the chosen image to the selected maximum side length, preserving its aspect ratio, and convert its pixels to grayscale intensities. Call the resulting m × n matrix A.
  2. Compute the SVD once. Decompose A and order its singular values from largest to smallest.
  3. Truncate and reconstruct. Keep the first k singular values and their left and right singular vectors:

    Eckart-Young makes this a best approximation of rank at most k. Changing k reuses the same SVD; it does not run a matrix completion iteration.

Storage and accuracy. The truncated factors contain k(m + n + 1) numbers, compared with mn pixel values in the original matrix. The reported compression ratio counts numbers, rather than encoded file bytes; at large k, the factors can require more numbers than the original image.
