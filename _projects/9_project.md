Connected intelligence studies systems in which learning, communication, sensing, and computation are designed together. The motivating applications include edge-supported robotics, augmented and virtual reality, real-time media, large-language-model assistants, and other distributed AI workloads that depend on reliable networked infrastructure.

This project reflects ongoing work through the Texas A&M Connected Intelligence Initiative and related sponsored research on resilient AI with network and edge synergism. The technical focus is on AI systems that continue to operate when bandwidth, compute, energy, security, or infrastructure conditions are constrained.

Research directions include resilient next-generation wireless systems, edge/cloud cooperation, AI-native communication protocols, and principled methods for deploying intelligence across devices and networks. These directions build naturally on work in AI-native wireless communications, over-the-air computation, unsourced access, and semantic communication.

The long-term objective is to make connected intelligent systems dependable enough for real deployments while keeping the theoretical foundations clear enough to guide design rather than only benchmark it after the fact.
